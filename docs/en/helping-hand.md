# RibbitFlip as a helping hand

← [README](../../README.en.md) · [Polski](../pl/pomocna-dlon.md)

The capsule sits in your hand, but it also clips to the brim of a cap or to a headband, and then
your head drives the cursor. Several features were built for the moments when pressing a button
or reaching for a keyboard is hard or impossible.

- **Click without clicking.** Hold the cursor still for a moment and the click happens on its own.
  You set how long to wait and how much the cursor may wobble, so a tremor still counts as
  standing still.
- **A cursor driven by your head or your hand**, fenced to one monitor if you like.
- **A selection wheel instead of keyboard shortcuts.** Up to sixteen actions under one button, and
  the choice is on screen exactly when you make it. Five ways to drive it, from a lean alone to
  stepping through the slices with clicks.
- **Screen scanner: the whole computer with one signal.** A bar sweeps the screen, you stop it, a
  second one sweeps across, and the click lands where they cross. The signal can be a lean, a nod,
  a lean back, a held position, going still, a turn or a shake; none of them needs a press.
  Stepping back means a late signal costs one step, not the whole round. Same technique as Switch
  Control on Apple and Switch Access on Android.
- **A capsule you put down clicks nothing.** A capsule resting on a blanket or an armrest stops
  steering.

It helps whenever your hands are simply busy, too: at the workbench, while cooking, or watching a
film from the far end of the sofa.

How to set up click-without-clicking, the screen scanner and the selection wheel in detail: see the [feature details](features.md#feature-details) and the **About** tab of each module in the panel.
